My Journey

From writing my first Java program to building AI-powered backend systems.

This page isn't a list of achievements. It's simply the story of how I became the engineer I am today.

How It All Started

I was introduced to computers when I was in the second grade. At that time, they were nothing more than a source of entertainment for me. I spent hours watching movies, playing games, and exploring whatever I could find on the screen, never really thinking about what was happening behind it.

Everything changed when I reached the sixth grade.

One day, I found myself asking a simple question: "How does all of this actually work inside this box?" That single question sparked a curiosity that has never left me. I wasn't satisfied with just using technology anymore— I wanted to understand how it was built.

That curiosity led me to HTML, where I created my very first webpage. It was nothing more than a simple demo, but seeing something I had written appear in a browser was an unforgettable moment. It wasn't just a webpage; it was proof that I could create something from scratch.

From that day onward, I knew what I wanted to do. I decided that I would study Computer Science and dedicate myself to learning how software is built. That decision became one of the most important turning points in my life

College: Where the Real Journey Began

Although my curiosity started much earlier, my real journey into computer science began in college.

Like many students, my first programming language was C. To be honest, I didn't fully understand what I was doing at first. There was no clear roadmap, no mentor explaining how to think like a programmer, and no one teaching me how to build logic instead of simply writing code. Most of the time, I was figuring things out on my own.

It was frustrating in the beginning. I could write programs by following examples, but I often found myself wondering why the code worked instead of just how to write it. Over time, through constant practice, debugging, and making countless mistakes, the concepts slowly started to make sense. Every error became a lesson, and every successful program gave me the confidence to keep going.

Then I was introduced to Java, and that's when everything clicked.

For the first time, programming felt intuitive. I loved how organized and readable Java was, and how object-oriented programming encouraged you to think about solving problems in a structured way. It wasn't just another language to learn—it was the language that made software development truly enjoyable for me. Even today, Java remains one of my primary languages and the foundation of most of the systems I build.

As I became more comfortable with Java, I wanted to know how real applications worked beyond simple console programs. That curiosity led me to Spring Boot, where I began building backend applications and exploring the architecture behind modern software.

While building projects, I discovered concepts like REST APIs, the MVC architecture, authentication, database integration, and scalable backend design. At first, these ideas seemed like separate pieces of a puzzle, but as I continued building applications, I realized how beautifully they fit together. Seeing an HTTP request travel through controllers, services, repositories, and databases before returning a response was fascinating. Every new concept connected to another, and suddenly the bigger picture of software engineering became clear.

That experience changed the way I looked at programming. I was no longer just writing code—I was learning how complete systems are designed, how different technologies communicate with each other, and how ideas become real applications that people can use.

Discovering AI

While I was enjoying backend engineering, another field was evolving at an incredible pace—Artificial Intelligence.

At first, AI caught my attention because of how effortlessly it could automate tasks that would normally take people hours to complete. It wasn't just about chatbots or generating text; it was about building systems that could understand information, make decisions, and assist people in solving real problems. Watching that transformation made me realize one thing: the future of software engineering would be closely connected with AI.

I knew I couldn't afford to be left behind.

So I started learning everything I could. I attended lectures on Machine Learning, explored how data is collected, cleaned, and transformed before it ever reaches a model, and learned how different algorithms are trained and evaluated. Understanding the entire pipeline—from raw data to a working model—gave me a much deeper appreciation of what goes into building intelligent systems.

As Large Language Models became more capable, my curiosity shifted again. I wanted to understand not just how to use them, but how they actually work and how they could be integrated into real applications. I learned about embeddings, vector databases, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), prompt engineering, and the architecture behind modern LLM-powered systems.

Eventually, I realized that what excited me the most wasn't training models from scratch—it was building intelligent software around them.

Today, I combine AI with backend engineering to create applications that are both reliable and intelligent. My focus is on designing systems where AI becomes one component of a larger architecture rather than the entire application. I enjoy thinking about how APIs, databases, business logic, and AI services work together to solve real-world problems.

The area that fascinates me the most is Agentic AI. The idea of multiple AI agents collaborating, reasoning, using tools, and completing complex workflows feels like the next evolution of software engineering. It's a field I'm actively exploring, experimenting with, and building projects in because I believe it represents the future of intelligent applications.

Looking ahead, my goal is simple: to become an engineer who can build scalable backend systems powered by AI—not just applications that use AI, but systems where intelligent agents and robust software engineering work seamlessly together.
